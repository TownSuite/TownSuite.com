How to use it

  1. Type your question in the box. Plain language is fine — full sentences, fragments, either way. Press Enter to send. Or tap one of the suggested starters inside the chat to get going.
  2. Keep going with follow-ups. It remembers the conversation, so you can drill in: “what about for a town of 4,000?”, “how does that compare to what we do now?”, “show me the module that handles it.”
  3. Give it your context. Province, population, the department or process you care about, what you run today — the more specific you are, the sharper the answer.
  4. Ask for a demo whenever you’re ready. Say “book a demo” and it opens our booking form right there. Clear in the chat header starts a fresh conversation.

Worth knowing

Your questions stay in Canada

The model and your prompt data run on operationally sovereign Canadian infrastructure, alongside mERP® data. Why that matters: the Government of Canada’s white paper on data sovereignty and public cloud and its current Digital Sovereignty Framework.

Strictly about TownSuite

This demo exists for one purpose: learning about TownSuite. It answers from TownSuite’s public product knowledge only. Anything outside that scope is out of scope, and TownSuite Intelligence will abide by those rules rather than improvise. The full version answers from your municipality’s connected records.

It can make mistakes

Verify figures, timelines and anything contractual with our team before you rely on it.

Don’t share private data

Keep personal, financial or confidential municipal information out of the chat. For account-specific help, contact us or sign in to the portal.

Beyond this chat

The full TownSuite Intelligence

What you’re talking to here is the public preview: it knows about TownSuite. The full TownSuite Intelligence is a contextually aware, role-based assistant — each user automatically gets the skills, projects and context their role needs — that runs on your municipality’s own data — focused on expanding the context to suit your needs, for TownSuite and beyond. That context is self-healing: it improves over time through feedback and updated information, whether that arrives by ingestion, MCP, or someone on your team simply correcting it. Everything below is documented in the knowledge base.

  • Runs on your data, sovereign

    Answers in plain language only from your municipality’s connected records. Runs in Canada on Canadian-controlled infrastructure, encrypted in transit and at rest with keys in Canada. Never used to train models; nothing sent to a foreign AI service.

  • Role-based inference

    It knows who it’s working with. A finance officer, a planner, a clerk and an IT lead each automatically get the skills, projects and context that fit their role — so the assistant already has the right procedures, files and domain knowledge in hand before they ask, rather than everyone working from the same generic prompt. Access follows roles, so people only see the context they’re entitled to.

  • Documents & screenshots

    Attach files or screenshots and it explains, summarises, drafts, or works through the problem with you.

  • Folder work (desktop)

    Point it at a folder: it reads and changes files and runs commands — with approval prompts, allowed-command lists, and undo.

  • CLI

    The same assistant from a terminal: same sign-in, models, conversations and working-folder tools. Scriptable via flags (--y, --allow-edits, --project, --workspace); tsintel gui opens the desktop app.

  • Collaborative projects

    A shared workspace of instructions, files and skills every member gets automatically; add members by user id or by role. Files have version history showing who added each version; work the assistant saves is credited to the model alongside the person.

  • Skills

    Saved instructions it follows whenever relevant — how you format minutes, what a report must satisfy. Personal or project-wide, with a plain-language trigger and an on/off switch.

  • Memory

    Remembers durable facts about you and how you work. Review, edit or delete anything in Settings → User Memory.

  • Voice

    Replies read aloud with a previewable voice picker, and dictation instead of typing.

  • Generated files

    Files appear in the reply that produced them, with Open and Save buttons.

  • Share & edit chats

    Share a chat by link or download it. Reword a sent message and resend, keeping both versions.

  • Desktop, browser & mobile

    Self-updating desktop app for Windows x64 and Mac (Apple Silicon); browser version at your server URL; iOS and Android apps.

  • Bring in more context

    Securely ingest or stream other information via TownSuite Intelligence MCP and other ingestion methods.

  • Context that heals itself

    Its understanding of your municipality isn’t fixed at setup. Feedback on answers, freshly ingested or streamed data, and manual corrections by your team all update the shared context — so it gets more accurate the longer you use it.
