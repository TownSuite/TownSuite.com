Reachable by foreign authorities

Residency isn't protection.

When your data sits with a U.S.-owned provider — even in a Canadian datacentre — American authorities can compel that provider to produce it. Anywhere in the world. Including Canada.

Sovereign in name only

Your jurisdiction is set by the weakest layer.

A Canadian logo on the contract isn't enough. If any layer of the stack — the application, the platform, or the infrastructure it runs on — belongs to a foreign-owned company, that layer answers to foreign law and takes your data's jurisdiction with it. Ask every vendor who can access, operate, or be compelled to produce each layer.

Reachable by foreign authorities — under any flag

It's not just the U.S. — every country's cloud is subject to its country's laws.

The U.S. CLOUD Act is the famous one — but Australia, Germany, the U.K. and most major jurisdictions have laws that reach through their companies. Whichever country owns a layer of your stack, its law comes with it — even when the provider builds a dedicated “Canadian cloud” or sovereign region here. The datacentre moves; the legal obligations of its owner don't. The answer isn't a better foreign cloud — it's a stack under sovereign Canadian control, where no foreign entity can operate it or be compelled to hand it over.

Outside foreign reach — by contract & architecture

Sovereignty is in our DNA.

TownSuite is 100% Canadian owned, and Cirrus, Micrologic's cloud, is Canadian operated. Our contract with Cirrus contractually commits to Canadian operational sovereignty: the infrastructure your data runs on is located, operated and administered exclusively in Canada, and is governed exclusively by Canadian law — so the jurisdiction your data answers to stays in Canada.
